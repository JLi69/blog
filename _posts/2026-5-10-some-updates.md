## My Thoughts on University (after a year)

I finished finals a few days ago (I think I kind of finished a few days early
for my university so that's kind of lucky) so my summer break has officially
started. Since I'm now done with my first year of college, I think it's fair
that I can give my thoughts about what happened and maybe do some reflecting.

I would say that university was definitely a significantly different experience
to what I was previously used to in middle school and high school. In some ways
it was more relaxing as the classes didn't take up as large of a portion of the
day. Usually in high school I was at school from 8 AM to 3 PM, 7 hours, but with
university I could spend time in my dorm and also just had less classes and that
cut the time that I was in classes to 2 - 4 hours, depending on the day. This
was relaxing at times and certainly gave me some time to work on other things
(or to work on my homework) so that was nice in my opinion.

I would say that most of the classes that I took this year weren't too terrible,
I was able to understand the content and do the assignments on time and get
decent grades on the exam. I haven't gotten all of my grades for the second
semester as of the writing of this post, but for the first semester I got all
A's except for one class in which I got an AB (even then the grade was about
92%). As for second semester, I think I got A's in a number of classes, potentially
AB's in some other classes, but I don't think I fell below AB for any of them
as that would require a catastrophic failure on the final exam for me. So hopefully
I don't jinx that. However, I still do feel that I kind of underestimated the
difficulty of some classes. I was used to somewhat easy classes in high school
and when it was difficult, I was still able to get the hang of it after
a couple months. As for some college classes, I still felt I had to 'adjust' 
even towards the end of the semester. Maybe it was simply because at times I
didn't put in all the effort I should have and that lead to me 'falling' in
terms of grades and getting a little stressed at times. Essentially, I didn't
take some classes serious when I should have and I think that is a change that
I need to make through the rest of college if I don't want to completely flounder.
The first year was fine, but I don't know how much the second or third year is
going to scale up.

As for the social side, one major difference was that since I'm going to a larger
college, despite the larger number of people around, I feel more disconnected
to my peers in my classes than I did in high school. I knew most of the people
I went to school with in high school, and could recognize them in class but in
college, I might be lucky to see a couple people I recognize in a very large
class. I don't really talk to others in my classes and it kind of feels a little
isolating at times. However, I did still have a social life. I still spent time
with people I knew in high school and I tried branching out to meeting new people.
I did get to know some new people but I don't really feel as connected with the
larger community than in high school. I guess that just comes with a larger
school.
